# Five Original Concepts & Selection

Each concept was designed against the research gaps: world persistence, meaningful
choice, and pressure sources other than a literal chaser. No scores used — trade-offs
argued instead.

---

## Concept 1 — Arunika: The Last Light

- **Pitch:** The sun has not risen for a hundred days. You carry the last ember. Where you run, light blooms — permanently.
- **Core mechanic:** the **Light Trail** — every metre you run plants persistent bioluminescent flora in the world map. Lit regions stay lit for every future run, opening shortcuts, lore and safe zones. Light is also your health, currency and visibility — one resource, three pressures.
- **USP:** the world remembers your runs; the meta-game *is the level editor*.
- **World:** a mythic subcontinent-inspired dusk world (river ghats, banyan canopies, monsoon marshes, cliff temples) — original fantasy, no real religion or deity depiction.
- **Loop:** RUN → LIGHT → COLLECT LUMENS → BANK LIGHT at shrines → UMBRAL TIDE catches up → FAIL/ASCEND → GARDEN GROWS → RUN AGAIN (stronger routes).
- **Progression:** garden coverage %, ember ranks, spirit companions.
- **Risks:** persistence must not trivialize difficulty (managed by tide design + decay of old trails).
- **Feasibility:** high — trail data is a lightweight tile-stamp map; no complex physics.
- **Why different:** no researched game persists player-authored change *inside* the run space.

## Concept 2 — Tidebound

- **Pitch:** outrun a living flood that rewrites the level every 30 seconds.
- **Mechanic:** rising/falling tide reveals/hides routes; you surf your own wake.
- **Risks:** readability — dynamic levels punish casuals; hard to communicate "why did I die".
- **Verdict:** strong second — mechanics partially absorbed into Arunika (the Umbral Tide).

## Concept 3 — Emberlight Survivors

- **Pitch:** runner-meets-herding: shepherd a growing crowd of survivors past hazards; every death is a named loss.
- **Risks:** crowd readability on small screens; emotional weight could turn abrasive.
- **Verdict:** rejected for mobile clarity; revisit for PC.

## Concept 4 — Stiltfall

- **Pitch:** a *falling* runner — descend an endless megastructure, riding air currents, delay not gravity-avoid.
- **Risks:** inverted runner removes forward-readability, the genre's core skill; nausea risk.
- **Verdict:** rejected — the one idea that breaks the readability rule.

## Concept 5 — Rootwalker

- **Pitch:** vertical wall-running up (and around) a continent-sized tree, switching trunk→canopy→root layers.
- **Risks:** camera work is genuinely hard; motion sickness on mid-range phones.
- **Verdict:** strong art direction; traversal ideas folded into Arunika's banyan biome.

---

## Selection: Arunika — The Last Light

Chosen for the strongest combination of:

1. **Originality** — persistent player-authored light is absent from all 12 researched games; the fantasy ("my garden grows out of my history") is ownable.
2. **Depth** — one resource (light) drives visibility, health, currency and world-shaping; mastery = route authoring, not just reflexes.
3. **Feasibility** — lane-based 3D runner with a tile-stamp persistence layer; Godot-friendly; no exotic physics.
4. **Long-term potential** — seasonal Dawn events can re-theme the garden; asynchronous friend-gardens are a natural expansion.

**Trade-off accepted:** slower onboarding than Subway Surfers (persistence must be
taught) — solved by a scripted first run (see Bible §45–46) where the game lights
the player's first shrine *for* them.
